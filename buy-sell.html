Potential versus already finished

Renovate or move-in ready?

I spent years running numbers with investors. That same math protects you when you're deciding between a home with potential and a home that's already finished.

Four questions I run on every house

  1. Where is the ceiling?

    What the best finished homes on this street sell for sets the limit on what a renovation can earn back.

  2. What does this buyer pool pay for?

    In most of these neighborhoods, that means the kitchen, the primary suite, a home office and outdoor living.

  3. What is structural and what is cosmetic?

    Foundation, roof, drainage and HVAC come first. Paint and lighting are the easy part.

  4. What will the lot allow?

    Setbacks, protected trees, conservation districts and historic review can all shape the addition you're picturing.

Two transactions, one timeline

Sell and buy in the right order

Moving within Dallas means juggling two transactions. Here are four common ways to sequence them. The right one depends on your equity, your lender and your comfort with uncertainty.

Sell first

You know your exact proceeds before you shop, which makes your offer strong and clean.

Watch for: a possible short-term rental or a second move.

Buy first

You move once and on your schedule, often funded by a bridge loan or a line of credit on your current home.

Watch for: carrying two payments for a stretch. Open any credit line before you list.

Contingent offer

You buy subject to the sale of your current home, which limits your risk of owning two.

Watch for: sellers favor cleaner offers when a home draws a crowd.

Sell with a leaseback

You close your sale and stay on as a short-term tenant while you close on the next home.

Watch for: insurance and move-out terms need to be spelled out.
Rachael's take: Most of my clients do best when we prep the current home for market while we shop. Then we can move fast in either direction when the right house appears.
